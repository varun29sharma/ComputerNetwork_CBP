ZTNA · NIST SP 800-207

Identity-Aware Zero Trust Network Access Gateway

Invisible until you’re verified.

AegisX replaces the legacy VPN. Instead of “authenticate once, reach everything”, every connection is checked against who you are, which device you’re on, and what policy allows — and you only ever see the one application you’re entitled to, through a tunnel that is re-verified continuously and evaporates the second your session dies.

What is this?

Four moving parts, one rule: default-deny.

Control plane · the brain

Policy engine

Verifies your password, checks you’re entitled to the app you asked for, evaluates your device’s posture, then mints a signed JWT pass. It never touches your traffic.

:8000 · POST /auth/login

State store · the list

Redis

Sub-millisecond memory holding live authorization records plus the audit stream. It is the only thing the gate trusts: no record, no access.

:6379 · route:<ip>

Data plane · the gate

Netty TCP proxy

Sits in front of your applications and reads nothing from a connection until Redis authorizes it. Otherwise the socket is closed — silently, like a dead port.

:9000 · ZtnaAuthHandler

Continuous verification

The re-check loop

Every open tunnel is re-verified against Redis every few seconds. Logout, admin force-revoke, or TTL expiry kills connections that are already flowing.

every 5s · AEGISX_REVALIDATE_SECONDS

Comparison

Legacy VPN

  • ✗ One password gets you on the network — then you can reach everything
  • ✗ Open ports are visible and scannable by anyone
  • ✗ A stolen token stays valid until it expires, hours later
  • ✗ No path-level visibility: who is connected right now? Unknown

Comparison

AegisX (Zero Trust)

  • ✓ Identity + entitlement + device posture decided per login, per app
  • ✓ No authorization record? The port is invisible — scans get silence
  • ✓ Revocation kills open tunnels within seconds, not hours
  • ✓ Every allow/deny is logged; admins can force-revoke a live session

How a connection works

Identify → authorize → provision → enforce (repeatedly).

Step 1

You identify

Credentials plus the app you want and your device’s posture signals go to the control plane.

Step 2

Policy is evaluated

Entitled to that app? Posture claims acceptable? Denials stop here — before any tunnel exists.

Step 3

Pass + tunnel

A signed JWT is minted and, in one atomic Lua script, Redis gets route:<ip> = host:port EX 3600.

Step 4

The gate obeys — and keeps checking

Found → tunnel to exactly that app, re-verified every 5s. Missing → silent close. No record ever → invisible.
